# Visual Lineage

Visual lineage borrows transferable principles from human visual history without reducing movements, media or cultures to presets.

## Translate by channel

- composition — spatial masses, alignment, hierarchy, rhythm, negative space
- geometry — edge language, proportion, repetition, curvature, modularity
- typography — hierarchy, placement, expressive role, metadata systems
- color — field relationships, contrast, temperature, semantic use
- texture — material evidence, grain, print behavior, surface variation
- motion — pacing, direction, continuity, interruption
- imagery — crop, sequence, perspective, treatment and framing
- interaction — feedback, discoverability, system behavior
- symbolism — meaningful structural/symbolic systems; use with the highest contextual care

## Examples

`Ukiyo-e -> composition + imagery` may suggest flattened depth, aggressive cropping and active negative space without copying a famous print.

`Swiss Style -> typography` may suggest explicit type roles, alignment and information hierarchy without forcing Helvetica or sterile white space.

`Woodcut -> texture` may suggest carved directional marks and high-contrast material evidence without placing fake paper over every section.

## Anti-literalization

Never treat a movement name as a shortcut to a fixed palette, font, motif or decorative kit. If a reference remains recognizable only because of copied surface clichés, the translation is too literal.

For living, sacred, Indigenous or otherwise culturally specific traditions, avoid decontextualized sacred signs and identity-bearing motifs. Structural lessons are generally safer and more useful than decorative extraction.

## Counterbalance

References do not need to agree. Useful pairs include expressive/raw + editorial/systematic, ornamental/organic + geometric/disciplined, digital/retro + tactile/material, and kinetic/optical + conceptual/reductive. Assign conflicting references to different channels instead of averaging them into visual mush.
