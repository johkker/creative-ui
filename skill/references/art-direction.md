# Art Direction Reference

Art direction translates product meaning into visual behavior. It should answer four questions before implementation:

1. What should this interface feel like?
2. Where should visual tension come from?
3. Which conventions may be broken?
4. Which conventions must remain familiar?

## Prefer relationships over presets

Describe a direction through relationships such as:

- organic vs geometric
- polished vs raw
- sparse vs dense
- symmetric vs asymmetric
- static vs kinetic
- digital vs physical
- neutral vs expressive
- ordered vs chaotic

Avoid reducing a direction to a bag of effects or a named aesthetic trend.

## Extract principles from references

When references are available, extract transferable principles instead of copying surface details.

Good extraction:
- extreme typographic hierarchy
- marginal annotations
- image cropping as a structural device
- irregular pacing
- one dominant visual anchor

Bad extraction:
- use the same font
- copy the exact palette
- reproduce the same hero
- mimic the exact layout

## Visual tension

A useful design usually has controlled contrast between at least two forces: scale, density, alignment, texture, typography, motion, color, or spatial depth. Tension should support hierarchy and personality rather than reduce readability.
